# Sustainability Dimensions of San Francisco Housing (beyond flood risk)

Research date: 2026-11-04. All figures dated; "(snippet)" marks a claim taken from a search-result excerpt of the cited page rather than a full read of it. "Computed" marks a figure I computed by querying the cited DataSF dataset via its SODA API on 2026-11-04.

## 1. Retrofit/reuse vs. new construction (embodied carbon, office-to-residential conversions, Small Sites acquisition-rehab)

### Takeaway
LCA evidence generally favors reusing buildings over demolishing and rebuilding: the savings come mainly from not building a new structural system. Office-to-residential conversion in SF is heavily incentivized (Prop C transfer-tax exemption, waived inclusionary and impact fees, a 2026 tax-increment financing district) but still small: about 300 units in the pipeline as of Aug 2026, and none completed in the past decade. Acquisition-rehab (Small Sites) preserves existing housing, but current per-unit cost data is old (2018) and the program's total size is reported inconsistently.

### Cited Findings
**Embodied carbon / LCA evidence**
- Preservation Green Lab, *The Greenest Building* (2012) ran a 75-year LCA across six building types, multifamily included. Reuse saved 4–46% over new construction at equal energy performance. The exception: converting a warehouse to multifamily can have a greater impact than demolishing and rebuilding. Heavy material use in a reuse project can reduce or cancel its benefit. — [ACHP-hosted PDF](https://www.achp.gov/sites/default/files/2024-01/The_Greenest_Building.pdf) (snippet)
- The same study: an efficient new building takes 10–80 years to offset its construction-phase impacts through better operations. — [The Greenest Building](https://www.achp.gov/sites/default/files/2024-01/The_Greenest_Building.pdf) (snippet)
- In a NYSERDA/PCA (2024) comparison, a new building had 13 times the embodied carbon of an adaptive-reuse building, driven mainly by its new structural system. In the reuse case, windows were the largest embodied-carbon contributor. — [NYSERDA, Analyzing Embodied Carbon for Retrofit and New Construction (Apr 2024)](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/-/media/Project/Nyserda/Files/Programs/BOE/Analyzing-Embodied-Carbon-for-Retrofit-and-New-Construction_PCA_4-2024.pdf) (snippet)
- Statewide CALGreen includes an embodied-carbon measure with a mandatory pathway: either reuse 45% of the structure and enclosure, or do a whole-building LCA. — cited in [RMI Embodied Carbon Cities Policy Toolkit](https://rmi.org/embodied-carbon-cities-policy-toolkit/policy-examples) (snippet; not verified against the CALGreen text, and applicability thresholds such as non-residential size need checking)
- SF Environment Code Chapter 7 requires projects to complete an embodied-carbon strategies worksheet at schematic design and at project end. It sets no numeric limit. — [SF Environment Code Ch. 7 (amlegal)](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_environment/0-0-0-644) (snippet)
- SF's Construction & Demolition Debris Recovery Ordinance dates from 2006, and regulation SFE-23-01-CDO was adopted 2023-02-15. The city says it is "working toward" a citywide deconstruction policy; I found no evidence it has been adopted. — [SF Environment policy page](https://www.sfenvironment.org/node/3923) (snippet)

**Office-to-residential conversion: legislation and incentives**
- The Commercial-to-Residential Adaptive Reuse Program was created July 2023 (Planning Code §210.5). The Planning page cites both Ordinance 159-23 and Ordinance 122-23 for it, so verify the number. — [SF Planning, Downtown Adaptive Reuse Program](https://sfplanning.org/project/downtown-adaptive-reuse-program)
- Prop C (March 2024) waives the real property transfer tax on the first transfer after conversion, capped at the first 5 million sq ft converted. Approval must come before 2030. — [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/project/downtown-adaptive-reuse-program); [SPUR voter guide](https://www.spur.org/voter-guide/2024-03/sf-prop-c-transfer-tax-waiver); [ordinance text](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/za/za_PropC_ordinance_text.pdf)
- Prop C also lets the Board of Supervisors reduce, suspend or repeal the transfer tax without a citywide vote. — [SF Standard, 2025-12-16](https://sfstandard.com/2025/12/16/san-francisco-transfer-tax-reform/)
- Board File 240927 (passed March 2025) waives inclusionary housing requirements and development impact fees for up to 7 million sq ft of eligible conversions. Projects may add up to 33% more volume. — [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/project/downtown-adaptive-reuse-program). The effective date is 2025-04-07 per [Planning Director Bulletin No. 12](https://sfplanning.org/resource/planning-director-bulletin-no-12-downtown-commercial-residential).
- The Board vote was 9–1 (Supervisor Walton against). The ordinance removes the program's 2028-12-31 application deadline, and city staff estimate savings of about $70,000–$90,000 per unit. — [CBS SF, 2025-03-05](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/sf-supes-pass-amendment-ease-office-to-housing-conversions)
- A Downtown Revitalization and Economic Recovery Financing District took effect 2026-02-12 under state AB 2488 (2024). — [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/project/downtown-adaptive-reuse-program). ABC7 describes the developer benefit as a share of annual property-tax revenue for 30 years. — [ABC7, 2026-08-25](https://abc7news.com/post/san-francisco-office-housing-projects-are-gaining-traction/19668865/)
- Conversions are exempt from SF's all-electric major-renovation rule (they may use gas) if they convert before 2031-01-01. — [sf.gov All-electric buildings](https://www.sf.gov/all-electric-buildings-in-san-francisco)

**Office-to-residential conversion: pipeline (as of 2026)**
- About 300 units were in SF's conversion pipeline, versus about 15,000 in NYC and about 8,500 in DC. No office-to-residential conversion has been completed in SF in the past decade. — [ABC7, 2026-08-25](https://abc7news.com/post/san-francisco-office-housing-projects-are-gaining-traction/19668865/)

  - 901 Market St

  — [ABC7, 2026-08-25](https://abc7news.com/post/san-francisco-office-housing-projects-are-gaining-traction/19668865/)
- Planning approved 901 Market St (Hudson Pacific) for 136 units after about four months of review. — [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/san-francisco/multifamily/hpp-advances-office-to-residential-conversion-at-historic-market-street-property) (snippet; article date not confirmed)
- In May 2025 there was only one conversion in SF's pipeline. — [Mission Local, May 2025](https://missionlocal.org/2025/05/sf-offices-housing-conversion/) (snippet; outdated)
- A RentCafe figure of "8 projects / 825 units" covers the SF *metro* area, not the city. — reported via [search summary of Axios, 2026-07-13](https://www.axios.com/local/san-francisco/2026/07/13/sf-office-conversion-tax-incentives-zoning-downtown) (Axios page returned 403, so unverified)
- The Controller's analysis questioned whether the transfer-tax cut closes the feasibility gap for most conversions. — [SF Public Press](https://www.sfpublicpress.org/proposition-c-real-estate-transfer-tax-exemption-and-office-space-allocation/) (snippet)

**Acquisition-and-rehab (Small Sites Program, SSP)**
- SSP makes acquisition and preservation loans for multifamily rental buildings. After acquisition, annual rent increases are capped at 2–3.5%. — [sf.gov About Small Sites](https://www.sf.gov/information--about-small-sites-program)
- Funding: the Housing Trust Fund, plus a mandatory 10% set-aside of affordable-housing fees for buildings under 25 units. — [SPUR "Small Sites, Big Impact" (2017-01-31)](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2017-01/2017.01.31%20Small%20Sites%20Big%20Impact.pdf) (outdated). MOHCD's 2022 reform memo cites $260.7M in GO bonds. — [MOHCD Preservation Program Guidelines Reform Memo (2022-09-09)](https://sf.gov/sites/default/files/2022-11/Approved%20Preservation%20Program%20Guidelines%20Reform%20Memo%20-%20Loan%20Committee%209-9-2022.pdf) (snippet)
- Cost per unit (as of Feb 2018, outdated): average acquisition $360,568 per unit, excluding closing and rehab costs, and average rehab $65,339 per unit, excluding contingency. — [SF Planning Community Stabilization: Small Sites](https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/projects/community-stabilization/small-sites-program.htm) (snippet)
- Subsidy caps (2019 NOFA): $375k per unit for 5–9-unit buildings and $300k per unit for 10–25-unit buildings. — [MOHCD Small Sites NOFA 2019](https://sfmohcd.org/sites/default/files/Documents/MOH/Small%20Sites/Small%20Sites%20NOFA-2019-final.pdf)
- New-construction comparison: the Budget & Legislative Analyst found affordable new construction takes 5+ years and costs $800k–$1M per unit. The same audit called SSP the *most expensive* local subsidy program per unit, which presumably means city subsidy per unit, not total development cost (unverified). — [SF Chronicle (c. 2023)](https://www.sfchronicle.com/sf/article/affordable-housing-sf-funding-audit-problems-17878614.php) (snippet; paywalled)
- Program scale is reported inconsistently:
  - "47 buildings / 368 units" — [sf.gov](https://sf.gov/es/node/661) (snippet, undated)
  - "35 buildings / 290 units + 15 / 137 in pipeline" — [sf.gov Richmond release](https://www.sf.gov/news/mayor-london-breed-announces-acquisition-and-preservation-affordable-housing-richmond) (snippet)
  - "over $378M, 69 buildings, 843 units", a figure that may include programs beyond SSP — [MEDA](https://medasf.org/?p=46888) (snippet, undated)
- Computed: the MOHCD pipeline dataset currently lists only 2 Small Sites projects (11 units) in rehab. It also lists 4 "Big Sites" projects (269 units), 2 HSH Homekey projects (230 units) and 12 Multifamily Rental Program rehab projects (690 units). Completed acquisitions drop out of a pipeline dataset, so this is not a cumulative total. — [DataSF aaxw-2cb8](https://data.sf.gov/d/aaxw-2cb8)

### Inferences
- For a tool, the embodied-carbon case for reuse can be stated qualitatively with these citations. SF-specific, per-building embodied-carbon numbers do not exist in open data.
- Conversions are a marginal housing-supply channel (hundreds of units) compared with the 82,000-unit Housing Element target ([CBS SF](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/sf-supes-pass-amendment-ease-office-to-housing-conversions)). The inclusionary waiver means conversions produce no on-site affordable units by design. That is a trade-off between affordability and reuse.
- The gas exemption for conversions until 2031 means some reuse projects may lock in fossil systems. This pulls against the electrification goal.

### Gaps
- No official 2026 Planning count of conversion applications, approvals or exemption certificates. The Planning "Projects" dataset (qvu5-m3a2) could be filtered for change-of-use records, but I did not do this.
- No current (2024–2026) SSP total or per-unit cost. MOHCD annual reports were not reached.
- No SF-specific LCA comparing acquisition-rehab with new affordable construction.

## 2. Building electrification and energy

### Takeaway
New construction in SF has had to be all-electric since 2021-06-01. From 2026-07-01, major renovations that replace building-wide heating or hot-water systems must also go all-electric. 100% affordable housing is exempt until 2027-07-01 and can request a cost-based exception until 2031. Existing large buildings face benchmarking and audit requirements (EBEPO), not mandates to electrify. Tenant energy costs flow through utility allowances, which are a known barrier to electrification.

### Cited Findings
- **All-electric new construction:**
  - Ordinance passed 2020 (Ord. 8-20): [sfbos o0008-20](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/o0008-20.pdf).
  - Permit applications filed on or after 2021-06-01 must be fully electric (SF Building Code §106A.1.17.1). Exceptions are handled under Administrative Bulletin 112, and buildings must remain all-electric after completion.
  
  — [sf.gov All-electric buildings](https://www.sf.gov/all-electric-buildings-in-san-francisco)
- A BIC review reported about 300 applications under the new-construction rule and no feasibility-exemption requests. — [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/carbon-free-buildings/san-francisco-renovations-gas-ban) (snippet)
- **Major Renovations Ordinance (Ord. 174-25):** applies to projects permitted after 2026-07-01 when both of the following are true:
  - it is a major renovation (significant structural or non-structural alteration, or a substantial-improvement addition);
  - it replaces building-wide space or water heating, or installs systems serving at least 80% of conditioned floor area.
  
  Exemptions:
  - technical infeasibility;
  - DOE-preempted appliances;
  - commercial kitchens;
  - conversions to housing before 2031-01-01;
  - **100% affordable housing until 2027-07-01**, with a cost-based exception available until 2031-01-01.
  
  — [sf.gov](https://www.sf.gov/all-electric-buildings-in-san-francisco); [Ord. 174-25](https://www.sfbos.org/sites/default/files/o0174-25.pdf)
- Critics argue the ordinance raises housing costs. — [Reason, 2025-08-12](https://reason.com/2025/08/12/san-franciscos-all-electric-ordinance-will-make-housing-more-expensive/printer/) (opinion)
- **EBEPO (Environment Code Ch. 20):** annual ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager benchmarking for non-residential buildings of 10,000 sq ft or more and residential buildings of 50,000 sq ft or more, plus periodic energy audits for non-residential buildings. — [DataSF 96ck-qcfe metadata](https://data.sf.gov/d/96ck-qcfe)
- Computed for benchmark year 2025: 2,487 buildings in the dataset, including 190 "Multifamily" (mean ENERGY STAR score 85.8) and 426 "Mixed Residential" (85.4). Coverage grew from 1,526 buildings (2011) to 2,487 (2025). — [DataSF 96ck-qcfe](https://data.sf.gov/d/96ck-qcfe)
- **Climate Action Plan building targets:** the 2021 CAP targets zero-emission large commercial buildings by 2035 and all buildings by 2040, with net-zero citywide by 2040. — [SF Environment CAP summary](https://www.sfenvironment.org/CAP-2025) and secondary summaries (snippet); [2021 CAP full PDF](https://www.SFEnvironment.org/files/cap_fulldocument_wappendix_web_220124.pdf)
- A 2026 Climate Action Plan has launched. It covers seven sectors, including "Building Operations" and "Housing and Land Use", across 18 agencies, but the summary page gives no adoption date or targets. — [SF Environment CAP page](https://www.sfenvironment.org/CAP-2025)
- **BayREN multifamily (BAMBE) rebates, as of 2026-03-10:**
  - $7.6M unreserved for program years 2026–27;
  - base $500 per unit (2+ measures, 10% or more savings), plus up to $500 per unit for other measures;
  - electrification adders per apartment: $1,500 for an in-unit heat-pump water heater or HVAC, $1,000 for central heat-pump systems, $750 for electric cooking, $250 for dryers;
  - health and heat burden adders of $500 per unit each, for a possible total of $5,000+ per unit.
  
  — [BayREN BAMBE rebates](https://es.bayren.org/multifamily-property-owners/bambe-rebates) (snippet). Enrollment was paused for unreserved applicants from 2025-07-03 — [Marin County](https://www.marincounty.gov/departments/executive/sustainability/residents/multifam-res-energy-efficiency) (snippet). The two sources conflict on availability, so check current status.
- **SFPUC** publishes an All-Electric Multifamily Program Handbook. — [SFPUC handbook PDF](https://www.sfpuc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/All-Electric%20Multifamily%20Program%20Handbook.pdf) (not read; its terms are unverified)
- **LIWP Multifamily (CSD)** gives incentives for efficiency and solar PV in low-income multifamily housing, including heat pumps. FY2023-24 expenditure: $23.37M. Guidelines date from 2022-06-08. CHPC has flagged the lack of stable long-term funding. — [CSD LIWP](https://www.csd.ca.gov/Pages/Low-Income-Weatherization-Program.aspx); [CSD MF FY23-24 expenditure record](https://www.csd.ca.gov/Shared%20Documents/MF%20FY%2023-24%20Expenditure%20Record%20Final%20202501025.pdf); [CHPC LIWP brief, Apr 2024](https://chpc.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/LIWP_PolicyBrief_2024Apr.pdf) (snippets)
- **Utility allowances:**
  - A utility allowance (UA) is the estimated resident utility bill and is part of gross rent.
  - California owners mostly use the local PHA UA schedule or the California Utility Allowance Calculator (CUAC).
  - Owners are unsure how UA methods affect their operating income and residents' energy burden.
  
  — [CHPC, Decarbonization through Utility Allowances (2022)](https://chpc.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Decarbonization-through-Utility-Allowances-2022-Report.pdf) (snippet)
- A UC study modelled the bill impacts of electrification at three California affordable developments and treated outdated UA policy as a barrier. — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt63q9484p/qt63q9484p.pdf?t=rrm11z) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The 100% affordable exemption delays the cost burden of electrification for subsidized projects but creates a gap until 2027 or 2031 between affordable and market-rate renovations.
- For renters, the bill impact of electrification depends more on rate design (CARE/FERA, CleanPowerSF discounts) and UA schedules than on the equipment itself.

### Gaps
- I did not find TECH Clean California's current multifamily offering or its SF uptake.
- No SF-specific data on tenant bill changes after electrification.
- The 2023 CAP Progress Report and the 2026 CAP targets were not read in full.

## 3. Renewables (SOMAH, CleanPowerSF, Title 24 solar, community solar)

### Takeaway
SOMAH is the main solar subsidy for affordable multifamily housing (statewide, about 139 MW of a 300 MW goal as of mid-2026). CleanPowerSF offers low-income discounts stacked on CARE/FERA. Title 24 (2022) requires solar plus batteries on new high-rise multifamily buildings, with a community-solar compliance option. I found no SF-specific SOMAH count.

### Cited Findings
- **SOMAH statewide, as of mid-2026:**
  - capacity: about 139 MW completed or in the pipeline, against a goal of at least 300 MW by 2032-12-31, with about 58 MW operating;
  - savings in 2025: tenants about $58 a month (about 75% of their average bill) and owners about $1,977 a month (about 68% of common-area bills), per the May 2026 evaluation;
  - incentives can cover up to the full system cost; the program was authorized by AB 693;
  - participating tenants are auto-enrolled in ESA.
  
  — [CPUC, 2026-10-26](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/news-and-updates/all-news/bringing-the-benefits-of-solar-home---how-somah-expands-bill-credits)
- Completed SOMAH projects grew from 71 to 248 between late 2022 and January 2025, with about 360 more at the incentive-claim stage. — [CPUC SOMAH research plan](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/divisions/energy-division/documents/somah/evaluation-somah/somah3-research-plan_final.docx) (snippet)
- SOMAH data by project, which can be filtered to SF, is on California DG Stats. — [californiadgstats.ca.gov/charts/somah](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/charts/somah); [semiannual reports](https://www.californiadgstats.ca.gov/download/somah_progress_report/)
- **CleanPowerSF:**
  - SuperGreen Saver is a 20% bill discount with 100% renewable power for qualifying low-income customers in disadvantaged communities (CARE/FERA-eligible). At least 50% of project capacity must go to low-income customers. — [SFPUC news](https://sfpuc.org/about-us/news/cleanpowersf-now-accepting-applications-supergreen-saver-program); [CleanPowerSF DAC programs (2021-08-25)](https://cleanpowersf-sfpuc-yem2.squarespace.com/news/2021/8/25/cleanpowersf-announces-new-discount-programs-for-disadvantaged-communities)
  - CARE/FERA discounts apply to both the CleanPowerSF generation and the PG&E delivery portions of the bill. — [CleanPowerSF lower your bill](https://cleanpowersf-sfpuc-yem2.squarespace.com/loweryourbill)
  - CARE gives at least a 20% discount; FERA gives 18% on electricity for households of 3 or more. — [CPUC income-qualified programs fact sheet (2025-11-07)](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/about-cpuc/documents/transparency-and-reporting/fact_sheets/cpuc-income-qualified-programs_11072025.pdf) (snippet)
- **2022 Title 24 Part 6:**
  - solar PV is required on newly constructed high-rise multifamily buildings (4+ habitable stories); low-rise multifamily (3 or fewer stories) follows separate rules;
  - a battery is required alongside PV unless an exception applies;
  - multi-tenant buildings may comply via community solar;
  - adopted by the CEC 2021-08-11.
  
  — [Energy Code Ace 11.9](https://energycodeace.com/content/1197-code-in-practice); [CEC 2022 Energy Code](https://www.energy.ca.gov/programs-and-topics/programs/building-energy-efficiency-standards/energy-code-support-center/2022-4) (snippets). These are the 2022-cycle rules; whether a later code cycle is now in force and changes them was not verified.

### Inferences
- SF's dense, tall, shaded housing limits rooftop area per unit. This plausibly makes SOMAH and community solar more relevant than on-site solar mandates, but that is unverified for SF specifically.

### Gaps
- No SF count of SOMAH projects, MW or tenants (available from DG Stats; not extracted).
- No current CleanPowerSF enrollment numbers for SuperGreen Saver.
- I did not check whether the 2025 Title 24 changes the multifamily PV and battery rules.

## 4. Transit and walkability as a climate measure

### Takeaway
SF has screened projects with SB 743 VMT analysis since its March 2016 TIA guidelines: a project passes if its transportation analysis zone's VMT is at least 15% below the regional average. Most of SF screens out on this basis, which is why infill housing in SF is treated as a climate measure. Transportation is now SF's largest emissions sector (about 48% in 2024) and has fallen far less than building emissions since 1990.

### Cited Findings
- **VMT threshold:** under SF's TIA guidelines, a project is below threshold when site VMT is at least 15% below the regional average for each land use (residential, office, retail). The guidelines were adopted by Planning Commission Resolution 19579 on 2016-03-03, and the analysis is by transportation analysis zone (TAZ). — [SF Planning TIA Guidelines VMT memo](https://default.sfplanning.org/publications_reports/TIA_Guidelines_VMT_Memo.pdf); [TIA update memo](https://default.sfplanning.org/publications_reports/TIA_Guidelines_Update_VMT_Memo.pdf)
- **VMT data:** SF-CHAMP VMT estimates by TAZ for 2012 (existing) and 2040 (future), for office, residential and retail. Residential and office use tour-based analysis. — [catalog.data.gov: VMT estimates from SF-CHAMP 2012](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/vehicle-miles-traveled-estimates-from-sf-champ-2012) (2012 baseline is outdated)
- **Emissions (computed):** citywide GHG fell from 7.87 MtCO2e (1990) to 3.90 MtCO2e (2024), a 50.4% drop.
  - Buildings: 4.48 to 1.65 Mt (−63%; 56.9% of the 1990 total, 42.4% of 2024).
  - Transportation: 2.61 to 1.88 Mt (−28%; 33.1% of the 1990 total, 48.2% of 2024).
  
  — [DataSF btm4-e4ak, SF Communitywide GHG Inventory](https://data.sf.gov/d/btm4-e4ak) (updated 2026-10-21; years available: 1990, 2000, 2005, 2010, 2012, 2015–2020, 2022, 2024)
- The 2021 CAP targets net-zero by 2040. A secondary summary also gives a 2030 target relative to 1990 levels; the exact 2030 percentage was not verified against the plan PDF. — [SF Environment](https://www.sfenvironment.org/press/mayor-london-breed-releases-new-climate-action-plan-make-san-francisco-net-zero-emissions); [2021 CAP PDF](https://www.SFEnvironment.org/files/cap_fulldocument_wappendix_web_220124.pdf)
- Private vehicles are SF's largest source of emissions, buildings the second. — [KALW, 2025-05-27](https://www.kalw.org/bay-area-news/2025-05-27/san-francisco-environment-department-wants-feedback-on-its-climate-action-plan)
- SFMTA tracks climate goals and trends. — [SFMTA Climate Goals, Targets and Trends](https://SFMTA.com/node/16294) (not read)

### Inferences
- Because building emissions fell faster than transportation emissions, the marginal climate value of SF infill housing (lower VMT per household than the regional average) is plausibly larger now relative to building-efficiency measures. This is a qualitative inference; I found no SF-specific per-unit VMT-avoided estimate.

### Gaps
- I did not find a current (post-2012) SF VMT map, or the 2023 CAP progress report's housing and transportation metrics.
- I did not find a quantitative TOD emissions estimate for SF housing.

## 5. Seismic (soft-story program, tenant pass-throughs, older-building risk)

### Takeaway
The Mandatory Soft Story Retrofit Program is nearly complete. As of 2026-10-07, 4,718 of 4,958 listed properties (95.2%) had work complete with a Certificate of Final Completion, and 228 were non-compliant. Landlords may pass seismic retrofit costs through to rent-controlled tenants under the Rent Ordinance, subject to a cap and amortization. The exact current rule needs checking against the January 2026 rule amendments.

### Cited Findings
- **Status (computed):** "Work Complete, CFC Issued" 4,718 (4,715 plus 3 with a case variant); "Non-Compliant" 228; "Newly Added" 12; total 4,958. Data as of 2026-10-07, updated weekly. — [DataSF beah-shgi, Soft-Story Properties](https://data.sf.gov/d/beah-shgi)
- Earlier compliance reports: 80% in DBI's FY2019-20 annual report and 90% in FY2017-18 (snippet); more than 5,000 buildings were in scope. — [DBI Annual Report 2019-20](https://sfdbi.org/sites/default/files/DBI.AnnualReport.2019-2020.pdf); [DBI Annual Report 2017-18](https://www.sf.gov/sites/default/files/2022-09/DBI%20Annual%20Report%202017-2018.pdf); [ESIP soft-story page](https://sfgov.org/esip/soft-story) (now 404; snippet only)
- **Pass-throughs:**
  - Seismic retrofit work is treated separately under Rent Ordinance §37.3(a)(3). Costs certified under Rules 7.10–7.17 may be passed through without a fair-return analysis. — [Rent Board Prop H implementation regulation (archive)](https://sfrb.org/ftp/meetingarchive/index_311_0316.html) (dated)
  - For seismic work required by law, the pass-through cannot exceed 10% of base rent in any year, under a separate petition. — [sf.gov Capital improvement petitions](https://www.sf.gov/information--capital-improvement-petitions) (snippet)
  - Amortization was 20 years under Rule 7.12(c) for petitions filed from 2000-12-21. A 30-year amortization for seismic work was proposed in 2009, and I did not confirm whether it was adopted. — [Rent Board archive](https://sfrb.org/ftp/meetingarchive/index_313_6e76.html) (snippet)
  - January 2026 rule amendments replaced the 5-year capital-improvement amortization with 7 years. How this affects seismic work is unconfirmed. — [Rent Board amendments list, 2026-01-13](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/626A_Complete_List_of_Amendments_to_the_RRs_1-13-26_.pdf) (snippet)
  - Expedited hearing procedures exist for seismic capital improvements (Rent Ordinance §37.8B). — [sf.gov §37.8B](https://www.sf.gov/zh-hant/information--sec-378b-expedited-hearing-and-appeal-procedures-capital-improvements-resulting-seismic)
- Landlord-side sources describe 100% cost recovery via pass-through, including PACE-financed retrofits. — [SFAA magazine, Jul 2017](https://www.sfaa.org/Public/Magazine/07_2017/Rebate__Your__Retrofit.aspx) (landlord advocacy; dated)
- **Older-building risk:** the Seismic Hazard Zones map (liquefaction and landslide) shows where state Seismic Hazards Mapping Act site investigations apply. — [DataSF re79-p8j5](https://data.sf.gov/d/re79-p8j5)

### Inferences
- With about 95% of soft-story buildings retrofitted, the program's remaining tenant impact is largely ongoing amortized pass-throughs on already-completed work, not new retrofits.
- Other older-building seismic risks (non-ductile concrete, pre-1978 multifamily) are outside the soft-story program. SF's ESIP has studied them, but I found no current mandate in this research.

### Gaps
- The operative current text of Rule 7.12 and §37.7 for seismic amortization.
- The aggregate dollar value of seismic pass-throughs. The Rent Board petition data was not located on DataSF.
- The status of any concrete-building retrofit mandate.

## 6. Heat and air quality

### Takeaway
SF's heat risk is low in absolute terms but its health impact is high relative to other places: SF's heat-related ED visits rose more than almost anywhere in California in 2006. SFDPH's Heat Vulnerability Index dates from about 2013. Health Code Article 38 requires enhanced ventilation (MERV 13-equivalent) for new sensitive-use development in the Air Pollutant Exposure Zone (APEZ), which was updated in 2025. Ordinance 186-25 amended Article 38 in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Heat Vulnerability Index:** SFDPH's HVI (about 2013) uses 21 variables (individual, social, environmental) to map heat-wave vulnerability and associated air-quality impacts. — [US Climate & Health Alliance summary](https://usclimateandhealthalliance.org/post_resource/climate-and-health-understanding-the-risk-an-assessment-of-san-franciscos-vulnerability-to-extreme-heat-events); [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/em/disaster/San-Francisco-Health-Heat-Vulnerability-Index.html) (dated)
- In the 2006 California heat wave, SF ED visits increased more than almost anywhere else in the state. The Climate & Health Program informs the extreme heat emergency plan and trains older adults. — [CDC Climate-Ready: San Francisco](https://www.cdc.gov/climateandhealth/state-programs/sf.htm) (snippet)
- SFDPH also publishes a Flood Health Vulnerability Index by block group, built from socioeconomic, health, housing-quality and exposure indicators. It was last updated 2016-03-25. — [DataSF cne3-h93g](https://data.sf.gov/d/cne3-h93g)
- **HAQR Priority Green Infrastructure Zones** (Office of Resilience & Capital Planning, 2024-06-20): heat and air-quality priority zones where tree canopy is unequally distributed. — [DataSF nn26-kuy2](https://data.sf.gov/d/nn26-kuy2)
- **APEZ:** the map identifies areas of higher modelled air pollution. It must be updated every 5 years under Health Code Article 38, and a 2025 APEZ is published. — [DataSF t65d-x6p8](https://data.sf.gov/d/t65d-x6p8) (updated 2025-03-19)
- **Article 38 requirements:**
  - New sensitive-use development (e.g. housing) in the APEZ must submit an Enhanced Ventilation Plan; DPH enforces. — [sf.gov Plan for enhanced ventilation](https://sf.gov/plan-enhanced-ventilation-your-building); [DPH Article 38 guidance (2024)](https://www.sf.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/Article38DevGuidance%20%281%29.pdf)
  - Ordinance 186-25 amends Article 38 to clarify the requirements, with filtration equivalent to MERV 13 (ASHRAE 52.2). — [sfbos o0186-25](https://www.sfbos.org/sites/default/files/o0186-25.pdf); [BIC 2025-08-20 item](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/BIC_Meeting_8-20-25_Item_5_H2BM3ne.pdf) (snippets; effective date not confirmed)
- Tree canopy was estimated at 13.7% (Urban Forest Plan, 2013 analysis). That dataset is historical only. — [DataSF ni2e-vpbg](https://data.sf.gov/d/ni2e-vpbg)

### Inferences
- Enhanced ventilation in the APEZ adds mechanical systems to infill housing along freeways and arterials, which is exactly where VMT-efficient, transit-rich sites are. There is a trade-off between climate siting and exposure that a tool could surface by overlaying the APEZ with the housing pipeline.
- Article 38 MERV-13 filtration also offers wildfire-smoke protection. That is an inference, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- I found no current SFDPH HVI dataset on DataSF; the interactive map is at sfclimatehealth.org (not checked).
- No data on cooling or air conditioning in SF affordable housing, and no SF policy on maximum indoor temperature was found.
- The adoption and effective date of Ord. 186-25 was not confirmed.
