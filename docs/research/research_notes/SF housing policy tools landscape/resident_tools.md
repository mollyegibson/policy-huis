# Tools SF Residents Use to Understand Housing Rules and Tenant Rights (as of November 2026)

Method note: this is about 17 web searches and fetches done on 2026-11-03. Most findings come from search-result snippets and summaries. I opened only two pages in full: the sf.gov Rent Board department page and the Contra Costa court landlord/tenant page. The Stanford News article on the LA court AI tools returned HTTP 403 and could not be read. Items marked **[older]** come from sources before 2025 and may be out of date. Items marked **[unverified]** came from a snippet or a secondary source and still need a check against the primary source.

## Official sources: SF Rent Board, DAHLIA, MOHCD, 311

### Takeaway
The SF Rent Board is the main official source for rent-control questions. It offers topic pages in English plus four other languages, phone and in-person counseling, a forms center, and a property portal (portal.sfrb.org). I found no interactive rent-increase calculator and no chatbot on its department page. DAHLIA is the city's portal for affordable-housing lotteries, and eligibility is set building by building.

### Cited Findings
- Rent Board counseling by phone: 415-252-4600, Monday to Friday, 9am–12pm and 1–4pm. In person at 25 Van Ness Ave, Suite 700, Monday to Friday, 9am–4pm. Appointments can be booked at sf.gov/speak-rent-board-counselor. — [SF Rent Board department page](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board)
- Topic pages on the department page cover: which units the law covers, rent increase rules, eviction requirements, security deposits, roommates, owner move-in, Ellis Act evictions, buyouts, a referral listing (organizations, not individual attorneys), and trainings. — [SF Rent Board](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board)
- The department page has a rent-increase card (calculator icon) showing the allowed 2026–27 increase, but I found no separate interactive calculator. The department page also lists no "fact sheets" or chatbot. The Rent Board historically published numbered topic pages, and sf.gov hosts at least one numbered PDF ("571 allowable annual increases 25-26"). — [SF Rent Board](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board); [sf.gov file 571](https://sf.gov/file/571-allowable-annual-increases-25-26-en-1124pdf)
- The allowable annual increase is 1.6% for March 1, 2026 to February 28, 2027. It was 1.4% for 2025–26 and 1.7% the year before. — [sf.gov announcement 3/1/26–2/28/27](https://www.sf.gov/news--annual-rent-increase-3126-22827-announced); [sf.gov 3/1/25–2/28/26](https://www.sf.gov/news/annual-rent-increase-3125-22826-announced)
- The increase is set at 60% of the Bay Area CPI change. Increases a landlord skips can be "banked" and imposed later. A raise needs 30 days' written notice, or 90 days if it is over 10%. **[unverified: from a search summary of this page]** — [Learn about rent increases in SF](https://www.sf.gov/learn-about-rent-increases-san-francisco)
- The Rent Board publishes a "current rates" page covering the rent increase, relocation payments, and security-deposit interest. — [sf.gov current rates](https://www.sf.gov/reports--current-rates-including-rent-increase-relocation-sec-deposit)
- The portal (portal.sfrb.org) lets users "view information about residential properties," and owners use it to meet Housing Inventory requirements and pay fees. — [SF Rent Board](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board)
- The Rent Board website is available in English, Español, 繁體中文, Filipino, and Tiếng Việt, and "many" forms come in those languages. The department page does not say which languages counseling is offered in. — [SF Rent Board](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board)
- California's Tenant Protection Act has its own sf.gov information page. — [sf.gov CA Tenant Protection Act 2019](https://www.sf.gov/information/california-tenant-protection-act-2019)
- DAHLIA (housing.sfgov.org) is the city site for applying to rent or buy affordable units. Most listings use a lottery, and a few rentals are first-come, first-served. Each building sets its own eligibility rules. — [sf.gov: creating online affordable housing portal](https://sf.gov/information/creating-online-affordable-housing-portal); [Shirley Chisholm Village resident selection criteria, Jan 2026](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/Shirley_Chisholm_Village_-_Resident_Selection_Criteria_January_2026.pdf)
- DAHLIA was built with the vendor Exygy, and a case study describes the redesign. — [Exygy case study](https://exygy.com/case-studies/reimagining-the-affordable-housing-process-in-san-francisco)
- SF Planning publishes "A guide to applying for affordable housing in San Francisco." **[publication date unverified]** — [SF Planning PDF](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/citywide/housing-affordability/A%20guide%20to%20applying%20for%20affordable%20housing%20in%20San%20Francisco.pdf)
- SF Public Library held an in-person affordable-housing workshop for District 7 on February 12, 2026, which shows that in-person navigation help is offered alongside DAHLIA. — [SFPL event](https://sfpl.org/events/2026/02/12/workshop-district-7-affordable-housing)
- MOHCD published an "SF Housing Resource Guide" PDF listing counseling organizations. **[older: about 2019; some phone numbers and addresses it lists conflict with newer sources]** — [MOHCD SF Housing Resource Guide](https://sfmohcd.org/sites/default/files/Documents/MOH/SF%20Housing%20Resource%20Guide.pdf)
- The SF Human Rights Commission keeps a "Resources for Housing" referral page. It says the Eviction Defense Collaborative (EDC) runs Tenant Right to Counsel. — [SF HRC](https://sf-hrc.org/resources-housing)

### Inferences
- The official tools are informational: static topic pages, PDFs, phone and in-person counseling. To my knowledge none is interactive (no unit-coverage checker, no calculator, no chatbot). Residents have to work out for themselves whether their unit falls under the SF Rent Ordinance, AB 1482 only, or neither.
- DAHLIA's eligibility is set per listing (AMI percentage, household size). That makes it hard to answer "am I eligible?" in general without reading each listing.

### Gaps

- I could not confirm whether DAHLIA has a general eligibility pre-screener. One secondary site cited a 2025–26 100% AMI figure for a single person ($109,100), but I left it out because it was unverified. Use MOHCD's official income-limit tables instead.
- I did not research 311's role in housing (routing, housing-code complaints to DBI). There are no findings on it.
- I did not open the MOHCD resident-resources pages beyond the older guide.
- A Bloomberg Philanthropies PDF dated October 2026, "Affordable housing platform simplifying how residents find and apply for affordable housing," came up in search but I did not read it. It may describe DAHLIA or a similar platform. [bbhub PDF](https://assets.bbhub.io/dotorg/sites/83/2026/10/AFFORDABLE-HOUSING-PLATFORM-SIMPLIFYING-HOW-RESIDENTS-FIND-AND-APPLY-FOR-AFFORDABLE-HOUSING.pdf)

## Nonprofits and right to counsel: what self-help they offer

### Takeaway
Nonprofit help in SF is mostly human counseling: drop-in clinics, hotlines, and full legal representation through the city's Tenant Right to Counsel (TRC) program, which EDC leads. Written self-help is mostly know-your-rights pages, handbooks, and toolkits. I found no SF nonprofit running an AI tool.

### Cited Findings
- **Housing Rights Committee of SF (HRCSF):** free counseling for SF tenants in all housing types, including rent-controlled units, SROs, public housing, and Section 8. It serves more than 5,000 tenants a year. — [HRCSF about](https://www.hrcsf.org/who-we-are); [HRCSF know your rights](https://www.hrcsf.org/know-your-rights)
- HRCSF runs a drop-in Tenants' Rights and Legal Clinic in Bayview-Hunters Point on Tuesdays, 1–4pm, according to a site notice that mentions closures in late December 2025. Its job postings for a Spanish-language tenant counselor point to multilingual counseling. — [HRCSF](https://www.hrcsf.org/know-your-rights); [Idealist posting](https://www.idealist.org/es/ong-empleo/24ad6106c3934f1aae95548768653ec7-spanish-language-tenant-counselor-housing-rights-committee-of-san-francisco-san-francisco)
- **SF Tenants Union (SFTU):** volunteer-led and supported by members, at 558 Capp St, phone 415-282-6622. It runs a drop-in counseling clinic. Members receive a tenant handbook and phone counseling. — [SFTU](https://sftu.org/)
- **Tenants Together:** California's statewide renters' rights organization. Its call-back hotline is (888) 495-8020, staffed by advocates who "do not provide legal advice." It publishes an AB 1482 toolkit in English and Spanish with sample letters. **[older: a past listing warned of call-backs taking several weeks]** — [Tenants Together hotline](https://www.tenantstogether.org/es/node/3291); [AB 1482 toolkit](https://tenantstogether.org/resources/1482); [Toolkit PDF](https://www.tenantstogether.org/sites/tenantstogether.org/files/TT_AB_1482_Toolkit%20Eng_Spn.pdf)
- **Eviction Defense Collaborative (EDC) and Tenant Right to Counsel:** SF voters approved Prop F in 2018, making SF the first city with a tenant right to counsel. EDC leads the program and coordinates with partner legal services organizations. Late-2025 job postings show EDC hiring an RTC coordinator who refers eligible clients to the right partner. — [Next City on Prop F](https://nextcity.org/urbanist-news/a-win-for-tenants-rights-in-historic-prop-f-vote-in-san-francisco); [Idealist RTC coordinator](https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit-job/0386eca30ed54bdfb243b73c63f9b675-right-to-counsel-coordinator-eviction-defense-collaborative-san-francisco); [MOHCD TRC PDF](https://sfmohcd.org/sites/default/files/5.%20Tenant%20Right%20to%20Counsel.pdf)
- TRC outcomes: Bay Area Legal Aid (BayLegal) reported in June 2025 that the nine-organization TRC collaborative had a 93% success rate in the eviction cases it handled in the prior fiscal year. — [BayLegal, 2025-06-02](https://baylegal.org/tenant-right-to-counsel-prevents-homelessness/)
- Lawyer capacity is limited. Reporting from 2024 says there "aren't enough experienced tenant attorneys," and that whether a tenant gets a lawyer in California depends on where they live. **[older: 2024]** — [SF Standard, 2024-01-09](https://sfstandard.com/2024/01/09/california-evictions-tenants-right-to-counsel-san-francisco/); [CalMatters, 2024-01](https://calmatters.org/california-divide/2024/01/eviction-lawyers-california/)
- City funding: a Board of Supervisors resolution amended EDC's grant to $6,280,000, with an aggregate of $11,391,504. **[older: 2024 resolution; exact period unverified]** — [SFBOS r0001-24](https://sfbos.org/sites/default/files/r0001-24.pdf)
- **Bay Area Legal Aid (BayLegal):** its Legal Advice Line is 800-551-5554 and screens San Francisco eligibility. LawHelpCA lists the SF office at 1800 Market St, 3rd Floor, and the 2019 city guide gives a different address. The site posted a closure notice for May 12, 2026. — [BayLegal housing](https://baylegal.org/legal-areas/housing/); [BayLegal get help](https://baylegal.org/get-help/); [LawHelpCA listing](https://www.lawhelpca.org/organization/bay-area-legal-aid-san-francisco-regional-off)
- **LawHelpCA**, run by the Legal Aid Association of California, is a statewide self-help directory and information site that covers housing. — [LawHelpCA](https://www.lawhelpca.org/organization/bay-area-legal-aid-san-francisco-regional-off)
- **Causa Justa :: Just Cause:** only an older notice listed a phone number (415-487-9203). **[unverified, older]** — [Tenants Together SF page](https://www.tenantstogether.org/city/san-francisco?page=8)

### Inferences
- The SF tenant-help system is rich in people (clinics, hotlines, TRC lawyers) and thin on digital self-service. Each place a tenant can enter (HRCSF, SFTU, EDC, BayLegal, Rent Board) gives its own advice, and no shared triage tool connects them.
- Contact details in city guides go stale fast. Several numbers and addresses conflict between sources, so a curated, date-stamped directory would itself be useful.

### Gaps
- I did not verify Causa Justa's current SF tenant services.
- I found no current (2026) count of TRC cases or wait times.
- I found no nonprofit-published written self-help beyond know-your-rights pages and toolkits, such as letter generators or form builders, for SF.

## AI legal-aid and tenant chatbots (California, Bay Area, national comparators)

### Takeaway
I found no tenant chatbot from SF government or an SF nonprofit. In California, AI work is at the pilot or prototype stage: Stanford Legal Design Lab co-pilots with LA and San Bernardino legal aid groups, a Stanford and LA Superior Court project, Contra Costa Superior Court's beta chatbot "Coco," and a State Bar generative-AI readiness model. Deployed comparators are outside California: Housing Court Answers' Roxanne in NYC and Bailey B. in Florida. DoNotPay is the main enforcement warning case.

### Cited Findings
- **Contra Costa Superior Court "Coco (Beta)":** a "Beta AI Chatbot" on the court's landlord/tenant self-help page. It answers common questions and points users to court resources. The page gives no launch date and no disclaimer beyond the beta label. The same page says human self-help is limited to phone and email because landlord/tenant law is complex. — [Contra Costa court landlord/tenant](https://contracosta.courts.ca.gov/self-help/landlord-tenant) (page opened 2026-11-03)
- **Stanford and LA Superior Court (August 2026):** a Stanford team is working with the court to test tools for unrepresented tenants in eviction cases. They include AI error checks, legal-help referrals, and redesigned notices, tested in real-world trials. Another description calls it a randomized controlled trial combining paper notices, text reminders, and remote access to court documents. **[snippet only; the article returned 403]** — [Stanford News, 2026-08](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2026/08/ai-tools-tenant-cases-los-angeles-superior-court)
- A secondary outlet describes an "AI Default Judgment Assistant" pilot at the LA courts. **[unverified, low-authority source]** — [AI CERTs News](https://www.aicerts.ai/news/judicial-automation-la-courts-pilot-ai-judgment-assistant/)
- **Stanford Legal Design Lab:** it is designing and piloting four AI co-pilots funded by the Gates Foundation. Two are for eviction defense and two for reentry debt, with LAFLA (Legal Aid Foundation of Los Angeles) and LASO as partners. — [Justice AI co-pilots](https://justiceinnovation.law.stanford.edu/justice-ai-co-pilots)
- Stanford Legal Design Lab also built a 2025 prototype "Intake Agent" with the Legal Aid Society of San Bernardino for eligibility screening and fact-gathering in housing and eviction cases. — [Stanford LDL intake tag](https://justiceinnovation.law.stanford.edu/tag/intake)
- In Stanford's 2026 class, student teams worked on five challenges from housing, eviction-defense, and reentry legal teams. One tested flagging legal defects in a three-day eviction notice. — [AI for Legal Help 2026 class report](https://justiceinnovation.law.stanford.edu/ai-for-legal-help-2026-class-report-scoping-building-and-testing-new-legal-aid-tech-systems/)
- Stanford's lab runs JusticeBench, a hub for justice-tech benchmarks and projects. — [JusticeBench](https://www.justicebench.org/)
- **State Bar of California:** it launched a "Legal Aid Generative AI Readiness Model" and other tech resources through the Legal Aid Justice Technology Collaborative (LAJTC), with an updated PDF dated October 2026. It is a readiness and risk framework for legal aid groups, not a tool for residents. In 2024 the State Bar granted about $180.3M to 110 legal services organizations, with housing among the main practice areas. — [State Bar news](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/news/state-bar-launches-legal-aid-generative-ai-readiness-model-and-other-tech-resources-help-legal-aid-organizations); [Readiness Model PDF, Oct 2026](https://lajtc.calbar.ca.gov/hubfs/Legal%20Aid%20Generative%20AI%20Readiness%20Model%20(FINAL).pdf?hsLang=en); [State Bar legal aid funding report](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/news/californias-largest-legal-aid-funder-reports-628-million-investments-over-one-million-residents-assisted)
- The State Bar also publishes practical guidance on lawyers' use of generative AI. — [State Bar Generative AI Practical Guidance](https://www.calbar.ca.gov/Portals/0/documents/ethics/Generative-AI-Practical-Guidance.pdf)
- **DoNotPay:** the FTC's September 2024 complaint said the "robot lawyer" did not live up to its claims of replacing a human lawyer. The FTC approved the final order 5–0 on January 16, 2025 and announced it February 11, 2025. It requires $193,000 in relief and notices to 2021–2023 subscribers, and bars claims of equaling a human lawyer without evidence. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87474); [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/amp/articles/2296777)
- **Housing Court Answers "Roxanne" (NYC):** launched January 2025 to answer tenants' repair questions (documenting problems, letters to landlords, HPD complaints). It was built with NYU Law on Josef's platform, Josef Q, and is labeled "informational purposes only." — [LawNext, 2025-01](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/01/ai-powered-tool-launches-to-help-new-york-tenants-enforce-their-repair-rights.html); [Legal Technology](https://legaltechnology.com/a2j-josef-collaborates-with-hca-and-nyu-on-gen-ai-tool-to-help-nyc-tenants/); [Brick Underground](https://www.brickunderground.com/live/housing-court-answers-josef-chatbot-roxanne-answers-nyc-tenant-repair-questions)
- **JustFix (NYC):** a nonprofit making tenant tools that are not AI: a Letter of Complaint service sent by USPS certified mail, a Good Cause NYC coverage checker, and tools for building housing-court cases. A 2025 Cornell PiTech fellowship explored LLM chatbot tools for JustFix. — [Fast Forward on JustFix](https://www.ffwd.org/blog/justfix-nyc-housing-justice); [Cornell PiTech](https://www.pi.tech.cornell.edu/spotlight/empowering-nyc-tenants-to-navigate-housing-laws-with-llms)
- **Bailey B. (Florida):** launched early 2026 by Bay Area Legal Services (Tampa Bay, Florida, not the SF Bay Area) with LawDroid. It covers landlord-tenant issues and drafts basic documents, and is described as the third legal-aid AI chatbot built with LawDroid in the US. — [WUSF, 2026-02-23](https://www.wusf.org/economy-business/2026-02-23/bay-area-legal-services-ai-chatbot-tool-help-renters-know-rights)
- **Commercial or unaffiliated California tools:** Justee, a free California legal AI chat that says it gives "legal information, not legal advice"; CliveLaw, AI self-help for California evictions; and "Cali Tenant Counselor," a custom GPT. These are vendor pages with unverified claims. — [Justee CA](https://justee.ai/california/legal-ai-chat); [CliveLaw](https://clivelaw.com/evictions); [YesChat GPT listing](https://www.yeschat.ai/gpts-2OToXYBUw8-Cali-Tenant-Counselor)
- **Residents using general chatbots:** NBC News reported in 2025 that people using ChatGPT in court without a lawyer were "starting to win," including a Long Beach tenant who appealed an eviction. These are anecdotes, not a program. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/innovation/ai-chatgpt-court-law-legal-lawyer-self-represent-pro-se-attorney-rcna230401)
- **California chatbot law:** SB 243, the companion-chatbot law in effect since January 1, 2026, requires bot disclosure and gives users a private right of action. It targets companion chatbots, so whether it applies to legal-information bots is unclear. — [Cooley, 2025-10-21](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-10-21-ai-chatbots-at-the-crossroads-navigating-new-laws-and-compliance-risks); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/chatbot-compliance-wave-californias-new-law-not-only-one-watch)
- **Related SF AI housing policy:** SF banned "algorithmic devices" for setting residential rents, with civil penalties up to $1,000 per violation. It was reported as the first such ban in the US, in September 2024. This is about landlords' AI, not tenant help. **[older: 2024]** — [Local News Matters, 2024-09-04](https://localnewsmatters.org/2024/09/04/san-francisco-becomes-first-in-nation-to-ban-use-of-ai-algorithmic-tools-to-set-rent-prices/); [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/11998121/sf-moves-to-ban-ai-housing-tools-linked-to-price-fixing-in-thousands-of-rentals)

### Accuracy concerns and evaluations
- Stanford Legal Design Lab is interviewing landlord-tenant experts (court self-help staff, legal-aid attorneys, hotline operators) to rate AI answers to everyday tenant questions, and has said it will publish a report. Its quality rubric treats accuracy as "necessary but not sufficient" and says answers also need actionable next steps. It found the most harmful errors are procedural (wrong deadlines, wrong court names, wrong steps), and that too many disclaimers can stop people from acting. — [Interviewing legal experts on quality of AI answers](https://justiceinnovation.law.stanford.edu/interviewing-legal-experts-on-the-quality-of-ai-answers); [Measuring what matters: quality rubric](https://justiceinnovation.law.stanford.edu/measuring-what-matters-a-quality-rubric-for-legal-ai-answers)
- A Cyberjustice Laboratory study (Montréal) found ChatGPT strong on language but "not satisfactory" on accuracy, completeness, and reliability for landlord-tenant questions, compared with the rules-based JusticeBot. **[older: 2023]** — [CEUR-WS Vol-3435](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3435/short2.pdf)
- A New Zealand evaluation of "Rentbot," a tenancy chatbot from Community Law Wellington, included an expert accuracy review. **[older: 2020]** — [Otago report](https://ourarchive.otago.ac.nz/esploro/outputs/report/An-Evaluation-of-Legal-Information-Chatbots/9926480188401891)
- NYC's MyCity business chatbot reportedly gave advice contrary to city law, including on landlords and source-of-income discrimination. **[older: 2024; the only source found is secondary; the original reporting was by The Markup and was not retrieved]** — [Breitbart summary](https://www.breitbart.com/tech/2024/03/29/new-york-citys-ai-powered-chatbot-gives-businesses-disastrous-and-potentially-illegal-advice)

### Inferences
- The California pattern is court- and legal-aid-facing AI (co-pilots, intake, notice-defect checks) more than open public chatbots, probably because of unauthorized-practice-of-law and accuracy risks. The DoNotPay order and Stanford's finding that procedural errors do the most harm support that caution.
- No SF-specific AI tool combines Rent Ordinance, AB 1482, and the state rules. Commercial and general-purpose chatbots fill that gap with no official evaluation.

### Gaps
- **Courtroom5:** not researched or verified. No findings.
- **LawHelpCA or California Courts self-help AI:** I found no generative-AI feature on statewide courts.ca.gov self-help or LawHelpCA. That is not evidence one doesn't exist.
- **LSC grants:** I found no Legal Services Corporation grant for a California housing AI pilot.
- I did not retrieve the Stanford LDL report's numbers on how accurate models are on tenant questions. It may not be published yet.

## State-level tenant tools relevant to SF (AB 1482, DRE, HCD, AG, courts)

### Takeaway
State resources are static guides: the DRE / courts "California Tenants" guide, AG Tenant Protection Act materials, and courts.ca.gov eviction self-help. They don't tell a tenant whether their specific unit is covered.

### Cited Findings
- AB 1482 took effect January 1, 2020 and sunsets January 1, 2030. It caps rent increases and requires just cause after all tenants have lived in the unit 12 months or more. Exemptions include deed-restricted affordable housing and buildings less than 15 years old. — [Berkeley Rent Board AB 1482 page](https://rentboard.berkeleyca.gov/laws-regulations/state-law/ab-1482-california-tenant-protection-act-2019); [West Hollywood](https://weho.org/city-government/rent-stabilization/rental-housing/ab-1482-california-tenant-protection-act)
- The California Department of Real Estate publishes "A Guide to Residential Tenants' and Landlords' Rights and Responsibilities" at landlordtenant.dre.ca.gov, and a courts-hosted "California Tenants" guide exists. **[contents not viewed; URLs seen only in a Foster City handout and search results]** — [Foster City tenant info sheet](https://www.fostercity.org/sites/default/files/fileattachments/community_development/page/16212/fc_tenant_info_sheet.pdf)
- The Contra Costa court page links to the DRE Unlawful Detainer Guide and California Courts Self-Help eviction pages, which shows these are the state's standard self-help references. — [Contra Costa court](https://contracosta.courts.ca.gov/self-help/landlord-tenant)
- SF hosts its own explainer of the state law. — [sf.gov CA Tenant Protection Act](https://www.sf.gov/information/california-tenant-protection-act-2019)

### Inferences
- An SF tenant may be covered by the SF Rent Ordinance (rent control and just cause), by AB 1482 only, or by neither (for example, a newer single-family home). Telling these apart takes facts like the certificate-of-occupancy date, ownership type, and number of units. No official tool asks those questions.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve HCD tenant guides or a tenant-facing AG Tenant Protection Act page.

## Gaps residents report (fragmentation, language access, which law applies)

### Takeaway
I found evidence of capacity shortfalls (not enough lawyers, slow hotline call-backs, help limited to phone and email) and of rising demand. I found no systematic survey of tenants' complaints about information tools in SF.

### Cited Findings
- Not enough experienced tenant attorneys for right-to-counsel programs; access to counsel depends on location. **[older: 2024]** — [SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2024/01/09/california-evictions-tenants-right-to-counsel-san-francisco/); [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/california-divide/2024/01/eviction-lawyers-california/)
- Volunteer hotline backlogs, with call-backs that "may take several weeks." **[older]** — [Tenants Together hotline](https://www.tenantstogether.org/es/node/3291)
- Rising demand: Mission Local (September 2025) reported SF rents and eviction notices both rising during the AI boom, and SF Standard (August 2026) reported AI-driven rent growth, citing the city's chief economist Ted Egan. A syndicated report cites a 44% year-over-year rise in eviction notices from Rent Board figures. **[44% figure unverified; date unclear]** — [Mission Local, 2025-09](https://missionlocal.org/2025/09/sf-evictions-rents-rising-ai-boom/); [SF Standard, 2026-08-03](https://sfstandard.com/2026/08/03/ai-boom-san-francisco-economy-renters-rent-spike/); [AOL syndication](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/ai-boom-sending-san-francisco-140022000.html)
- Landlords exploiting rent-control loopholes, which shows how hard it is for tenants to know which rules apply. — [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/post/san-francisco-landlord-skirts-rent-control-laws-doubling-familys-pushing/19699525/); [CBS SF](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/san-francisco-landlord-accused-gaming-rent-control-loophole-veritas)
- Language access: the Rent Board site and forms come in 4 languages besides English, and counseling languages aren't listed. — [SF Rent Board](https://sf.gov/departments/rent-board)

### Inferences
- Help is scattered across at least 6 or 7 places (Rent Board, HRCSF, SFTU, EDC/TRC, BayLegal, Tenants Together, LawHelpCA, DAHLIA/MOHCD), and no shared triage step points a tenant to the right one. Together with complex coverage rules and only 4 non-English languages, this is the clearest gap an information tool could address. That conclusion is my inference, not from a resident survey.

### Gaps
- I found no SF resident survey or audit of housing-information tools. A Rent Board or MOHCD report or a nonprofit needs assessment may exist but did not come up.
- I did not research language coverage beyond the Rent Board. Russian, Arabic, and other languages SF supports under its Language Access Ordinance were not checked.
